Owner decisions, all on 2026-11-20 (SPEC §5 P11, ADR §0 and §0.1):

- **D1, cascade-bounded lazy.** Lazy maintenance is cascade-bounded, which narrows the per-node selection in SPEC §P11.
- **D2, Postgres.** Out of P11.
- **D3, GC.** An explicit `ctx gc`: a dry run by default, `--apply` to collect.
- **D4, layout.** Measured, and adopted only if it beats the threshold.
- **O1, lazy default.** Lazy is on by default. `E` is fixed in milestone 1 from the measured unit rate, aiming at a cascade ack of about 50 ms.
- **O2, read wait.** `read_settle_ms = 50`; Advisory reads do not wait.
- **O3, GC grace.** `keep_epochs = 10,000`, with a floor of 100.
- **O4, disk reclaim.** Offline, with `ctx gc --compact`.
- **O5, mirrors.** E4 is amended: GC does not raise `PayloadGone`, and a collected mirror body is verified by its hash and front matter.

## G5 — Budgets

Initial budgets, calibrated at the gate. All measurements are at 1M revisions on the local baseline machine. The remote baseline in ADR §1.3 is informational only.

- P1 budgets on multi_support and hub.
- Cascade commit ack (store, file projections) ≤ 150 ms on hub and chain with the default `E`.
- Total settle time ≤ 1.25 × eager.
- A deferred cascade's op_log frame ≤ 1 KiB.
- Reported:
  - seeding time for a base toggle and for a SetHead on a record that 10⁵ rules cite through Sem;
  - the next commit's ack after a cascade, with and without an idle gap;
  - `clone_fallbacks`;
  - the fraction of Certified reads that answer StructuralUnknown, on hub and chain;
  - `ctx gc` phase 1 and phase 2 times;
  - reopen time.

Layout candidates (ADR §6) are A1 radix heap, A2 packed rows and A3 pending vectors. Each is measured alone against the post-milestone-1 code on multi_support, hub and chain at 1M, 5 runs each. The noise band is the baseline's max/min spread.

A candidate is adopted only if:

- it is ≥ 15 % better on live heap or on cascade/settle time, outside the band;
- no metric is > 5 % worse outside the band;
- P1 and PROP1-lazy stay green.

Rejected candidates keep their numbers here.

## Milestone 1 — Core lazy maintenance

**Implemented** (`contextdb-core`):

- **One pass.** The counting pass and the L1/L2 status walk run as one interleaved, resumable pass (`lazy.rs`). Eager commits run the same code to the end.
- **Frontier.** It lives in `Db`, and `Db::drain` takes `Units`, `Rank` or `Full`.
- **At ack.** The ChangeLog is recorded and the epoch's history transition is opened. Changes are appended as nodes settle.
- **At full drain.** The transition is put in eager order. The derived-snapshot patch, the statistics (including `CommitStats.units`), DA5 and the rebuild check also run then.
- **Settledness.** `Db::settled` is the cursor test plus a backward search of at most `S = 4,096` nodes.
- **Unsettled reads.** `Db::node` withholds an unsettled node's state. `current_l0` returns `Option<bool>`. WHY, WHY_NOT and `diff` at the frontier epoch return `TxError::Unsettled`. `Snapshot(e)` at the frontier epoch takes the head from the inputs.
- **Refusals.** `Db` commits, `Db::dry` and `Kernel::run` refuse a frontier with `TxError::Unsettled`; debug builds panic.

**One clarification of ADR §3.2.** A fan-out entry counts as one unit when it is examined, not only when it is newly marked. Otherwise a node whose downstream incidences are already dirty could loop over its whole row without spending its budget. Seeding still counts only marks and pushes.

**Unit rate and `E` (O1).** Measured with `p11_cascade 1000000 3 <E> 1` on the local baseline machine (core only; informational, not G5 evidence). Each cascade is a base toggle of revision 0 on a 1M-revision shape and runs about 13.0M units.

| Shape | Eager commit | Units/ms (whole commit) | Ack at E = 750,000 | Ack at E = 1,000,000 | Ack + drain at E = 1,000,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hub | 1086–1193 ms | 10,896–11,970 | 31.9–47.5 ms | 43.1–63.5 ms (one 63.5 ms run, the rest ≤ 44.0 ms) | 1079–1196 ms (one run 2179 ms) |
| chain | 687–798 ms | 16,289–18,921 | 36.1–51.2 ms | 46.8–48.3 ms | 673–692 ms |

The first slice runs faster per unit than the whole commit, because it is mostly the cheap fan-out. **`E` is fixed at 1,000,000 units** (`LAZY_BUDGET`), which puts the core ack at about 43–48 ms (target ≈ 50 ms). The store's frame and projections come on top of this; G5 measures them in milestone 5.

**Tests.**

- `p11_prop1_lazy` is PROP1-lazy at 10,000 seeds. It runs in lockstep with eager `Counting`, `FullRecompute` and the oracle, with random `E`, `C` and drains.
- `p11_lazy_goldens` covers:
  - a hub that pauses in fan-out, with an unrelated node still settled;
  - the chain S-bound case;
  - P-1 through a Sem head;
  - commits and dry runs refused while a frontier exists;
  - `diff` and WHY waiting for settlement.
- On 1,000 seeds, 3,770 of 12,000 commits were deferred, with 7,059 drain slices and 19,316 unsettled reads checked.
- A deliberate fault, treating nodes up to 3 ranks above the cursor as settled, failed the property at seed 0.

**Results.** The full workspace (`cargo test --workspace --all-features --locked --no-fail-fast`) ran on the remote Linux host: 183 test binaries, 917 tests passed, 0 failed. PROP1-lazy took 48.6 s and the goldens 11.0 s.

**Deferred to later milestones.** The validate early return and T61 are M3; until then validate only ever answers negatively for an unsettled entry. The settledness memo in the published snapshot and the `Kernel`-level lazy commit and drain are M2/M3. The `Kernel::run` refusal is in place, but no kernel test can create a frontier yet.

## Milestone 2 — Store

**Implemented** (`contextdb-store`, plus the `Kernel` lazy API in `contextdb-core`).

**op_log format 7.**
- A frame body gains `settling` after `client_hash`. A settling frame holds exactly the acknowledged result, whose `l0_flips` and `status_diff` are empty.
- Format-6 segments stay as they are. Each frame decodes with its segment's format, and every checksum is read from the frame instead of recomputed by re-encoding.
- A segment of a lower format than the one before it is refused (`ChainBroken`, `SegmentHeader`).
- `migrate`, the `migrate5` check and `doctor`'s repair plan read logs that mix format 6 and format 7.
- `ctx migrate` 5 → 6 still writes format 6, so migrated checksums are kept.
- Kernel snapshots keep their own format 6, because their layout did not change.
- `ctx ui` takes a settling frame's diff from its replay.

**Version barrier.** A commit appends only to a current-format segment. The first commit after the upgrade rolls over to a new format-7 segment, synced before any frame. An empty older-format tail segment is rewritten in place under the same name.

**`Kernel`.** `commit_detailed_lazy`, `frontier` and `drain`. `drain` returns the settled diffs with global ids. Merges and the sole ops stay eager.

**Store.**
- `OpenOptions.lazy` (off by default; ctxd turns it on in milestone 3), `Store::drain`, `settle`, `take_settled` and `frontier_epoch`.
- The deferred commit's context is its own op_log frame: `drain` reads the frame back when the epoch settles and writes that epoch's projection rows from it, so the store holds no extra state.
- `settle` runs before each group's idempotency check, and in `what_if_detailed`, `impact_exact` and `snapshot`. It never runs in `replace_kernel`.
- A commit that leaves a frontier ends its group: the writes after it get no result and are submitted again.
- Open replays eagerly. For a settling frame it compares only the input-level result (epoch, allocations, net_delta, trailer).
- `changes` serves up to the last settled epoch, without `Truncated`.

**Open for milestone 3.**
- `TxnStatus` and commit responses do not yet carry `settling`; the flag is on the row (`LogRow.settling`).
- ~~Daemon readers of a frame's stored diffs see empty diffs on settling frames.~~ Checked in milestone 3: these readers use only `net_delta` and `allocations`, which a settling frame records exactly.

**Tests.**
- `p11_store` (8 tests) covers:
  - a lazy ack whose frame is `settling` and at most 1 KiB, with the projection watermark at e−1 and `changes` without `Truncated`, and whose settle equals an eager store, `diff_log` included;
  - the group ending;
  - the idempotent retry of a settling commit;
  - a crash before settlement;
  - a format-6 log continuing in a format-7 segment with its old checksums unchanged, read by `migrate` and `doctor`;
  - an empty format-6 tail segment;
  - the refusal of a format-6 segment after a format-7 one;
  - dry runs and snapshots settling first.

**Results.**
- During the work, mutations confirmed that the tests catch a missing rollover, a full compare of settling frames, a missing settle and checksums recomputed by re-encoding.
- The full workspace run on the remote Linux host (`--no-fail-fast`): 184 test binaries, 925 passed, 0 failed.

## Milestone 3 — ctxd

**Unit A, certificate validate (794aa97).**
- `validate_certificate_at` decides once which required heads are settled. Settled heads keep the eager path. An unsettled head gives only its input-level result (`Denied`, `Invalid{Changed}`, `Conflicted`) or `Pending`.
- Lag is computed over settled heads only.
- The `Ambiguous`/vector′ recompute runs only when every head is settled.
- `summary_at` does not evaluate observation lag for an unsettled node.
- Tests in `p11_validate`:
  - the owner's A(base)→B and B/C cases, N1 strict, issue on an unsettled entry, and issue/validate across a frontier;
  - the validate-contract property (rules 1–3, 10,000 seeds). Run against the milestone-2 `cert.rs` it fails, because stale `rules` came from an unsettled head.

**Unit B, the actor (07c8b53).**
- The store records `Step::{Commit, Drain}` in execution order, and the spare replays them with the same budget.
- While a frontier exists, `replace_kernel`'s debug check compares inputs and a frontier signature.
- The actor:
  - runs an idle drain of `E` units when nothing is queued;
  - serves `Message::Settle`;
  - publishes after any step;
  - requeues writes behind a settling commit at the head of its queue;
  - keeps a publication generation, which `wait_generation` and `pull` use;
  - lets the `ctx_changes` long poll wait on that generation.
- `<data>/ctxd.toml` `[maintenance]`: `lazy` (default on, O1), `lazy_budget`, `read_settle_ms` (default 50).
- Protocol 9: `Committed` and `TxnStatus` carry `settling`.
- Tests in `p11_actor` (8): ack and idle settle, the pull, FIFO requeue, 12 mixed rounds with 0 replay mismatches, retries, `lazy = false`, and an invalid config.

**Unit C, read paths.**
- One pull helper in service.rs. It runs the read on a snapshot. If the read needs unsettled nodes, it drops the snapshot, sends `Settle` up to their maximum rank (or to the end) and waits for a newer publication generation, then runs the read again. Side effects happen once, on the final snapshot.
- Deadlines:
  - Certified reads, certificate issue and validate: `read_settle_ms`.
  - Advisory reads send `Settle` without waiting.
  - The hook gate and the pre-push range check: 250 ms, half the hooks' default 500 ms timeout.
  - Mirror export: 30 s, the request timeout.
- WHY, WHY_NOT and DIFF at the frontier epoch answer `Settling{epoch}` on timeout. Mirror export never renders from a snapshot with a frontier. A `TxError::Unsettled` that still reaches a response becomes `Settling`.
- Tests in `p11_reads` (7) cover S13-rows-e2e (StructuralUnknown) and T61 through ctxd:
  - `ctx_check` is never `Valid` on an unsettled entry, and with the pull it answers the eager verdict;
  - the hook gate's check, `expect_cert_valid` and a `require_certified` merge;
  - WHY, DIFF and mirror export, during and after a frontier.
- The tests that check a completed pull use a 10 s `read_settle_ms`, so that the pull finishes on a loaded host. With the 50 ms default a debug build under parallel load once answered `Pending`; the release default is for the release daemon.

**Owner decisions (2026-11-25).** The owner confirmed the hook gate and pre-push deadline of 250 ms and the mirror-export deadline of 30 s (SPEC v1.11.1, SD14a).

**Open.**
- Advisory reads other than `ctx_read` and `ctx_recall` (briefing, updates, drift) send no `Settle`; the idle drain covers them.
- A response envelope's epoch is that of the request's first snapshot.

**Results.** The full workspace ran on the remote Linux host (`--no-fail-fast`): 187 test binaries, 949 tests passed, 0 failed. p11_reads took 49.2 s, p11_actor 1.4 s and p11_validate 73.0 s.


## Milestone 4 — Payload GC

**U2, core (38730ad).**
- One helper, `txn::live_again`, refuses `MissingBlob{content}` for `SetHead{new}`, `AssertBase`, every `AddRule` member, `Rebind{to}` and `MirrorAttach` of an existing revision. It runs on both the staged and the fast path.
- It also refuses a merge that makes a collected revision a head, a base or a rule member on the target. Bases are an addition beyond ADR §5.4's list: a merged `base = true` revives a revision exactly as `AssertBase` does. SPEC §6.2 was updated.
- Exempt: `Channel::Replica`, shredded contents (P9 rules), and a batch that carries the content itself.
- WHAT_IF and IMPACT take the store's presence: everything counts as present, but a collected content still refuses, so a dry run and its commit agree. Replay keeps `AllPresent`.

**U1, store (296e6c6).**
- **Key log.** Key positions are (segment, byte offset). Header kind 2 adds `collect_generation`.
- **Ledger.** `gc/ledger` holds (content, key_pos, generation, cutoff, now) records. A live key is one that no ledger entry names. Reads go Shredded → live record → `Collected` → `Missing`.
- **Two phases.**
  - `gc_plan` runs on a snapshot: K1 from every branch and sync state; K2 from artifacts, metas and notes (a log scan) and the nested document hashes; K3 from the frames in (cutoff, e0].
  - `gc_apply` runs in the actor: settle, re-check (e0, now], the pinned set (K4) and the held bound (K5); then the version barrier, the ledger append and sync, the pack index update and the key rewrite.
- **Failures.** Any error after the ledger append starts poisons the store; reopen re-applies complete entries.
- **Compaction and truncation.** Offline `compact` writes `compact/*.new` and renames it over the pack segment. It also writes a new ledger generation, prunes snapshots and checks PROP10. `doctor` refuses a truncation below the GC cutoff without `force`.
- The `nested` decoder runs on K2 payloads only. `ReviewDoc` is an artifact payload, and decoding every K1 revision would decrypt the whole store in phase 1.

**U3, ctxd and CLI.**
- **K5.** A `HeldEpochs` multiset, with a drop guard on every published and spare `Snapshot`. Its lock is a leaf lock, and it keeps no `Weak`.
- **K4.** The `vector` and `entries` revisions of every certificate ctxd keeps: session certs, root proofs, the session cert, pending handoffs, and up to 256 recently ended sessions.
- **The request.** `Request::Gc{keep_epochs, apply}` (protocol 9, store owner only).
  - A dry run reports the cutoff and its cause (keep_epochs or a held snapshot), the collectable count and bytes, and the kept counts.
  - `--apply` runs phase 1 on the connection thread, then phase 2 as one exclusive actor step. On `PlanOutdated` it plans once more.
- **Projections.** vectors, jobs and candidates each purge with their own `collected_through` mark, after every apply and at start.
- **`nested`.** `docs::nested_hashes`; its test classifies every document field.
- **Config.** `[gc] keep_epochs`: default 10,000, floor 100.
- **CLI.**
  - `ctx gc [--apply] [--keep-epochs N]`.
  - `ctx gc --compact` runs offline and refuses while ctxd holds the lock.
  - `ctx doctor` counts unavailable payloads by reason and reports a `Collected` head as a problem. `--truncate-before … --force` overrides the GC-cutoff guard.
  - `ctx team status` reports a `Collected` mirror head.
- **Team catch-up.** It now re-stages the replica's bytes for a `SetHead` or `AssertBase` naming an existing collected revision.

**U4, mirrors.**
- **The failure, as found.** At 296e6c6, `payload_gone` and the export refusal already checked only shredded contents. The failure was in `validate`: re-rendering an old tip turned a collected body into a placeholder, so after GC any cache miss became `MirrorModified`.
- **The fix.** `render_at` now fills each collected body from the tip's own tree. It accepts a blob only when `blake3(body) == content` and its front matter equals the re-rendered one.
- **The clean-cache mark** includes the GC generation.
- **`--rebuild`** refuses a collected head with an error naming its path. A current head is always in K1; it can read `Collected` only after a team catch-up whose replica lacked the bytes, which doctor also reports.

**Tests.**
- `p11_collected` (10).
- `p11_gc` in the store (8). It includes PROP20 at 1,000 seeds and a check that reviving a collected revision through the store's presence refuses until the bytes are staged again.
- `p11_gc` in the daemon (2): T67 through ctxd with K4 and K5, `MissingBlob` on commit and WHAT_IF, supersede back to the old text, compaction and restart under PROP10, and the keep_epochs floor.
- `p11_gc_cli` (1).
- `p11_mirror` (5).
- `p10_team`, with a catch-up re-staging test.
- Mutation runs during development showed each listed check is load-bearing: K3, the open-time key drop, merged bases, the catch-up fix, and the mirror's fill, hash and front-matter checks.

**Open.**
- The embedder can still write a vector for a content that a GC collected moments earlier; no later purge removes that row. Harmless: the row is never read for a collected head.
- `ctx reindex` does not clear vectors or candidates, so T67's reindex comparison is weak.

**Results.** The full workspace ran on the remote Linux host (`--no-fail-fast`): 192 test binaries, 979 tests passed, 0 failed.

## Milestone 5 — Layout ablation and G5

**Harness.** Build with `cargo build --release -p contextdb-bench --bins`. Every run prints one line, ending in `errors=N`, and exits 1 when `N > 0`. An error is a failed operation or a failed work check; stderr describes each one. A line that does not end in `errors=0` is not evidence.

- **Ablation**, one shape per process: `p11_ablation <multi_support|hub|chain> [n=1000000] [samples=1000]`, on a `Db<Counting, CommitStats>` with the generator dropped.
  - `load_ms`: the load. `live_heap_B_per_incidence`: live requested heap (stats_alloc, as `contextdb-bench`) per shape incidence, after the commit samples.
  - `commit_p99_ns`: `samples` SetRuleEnabled commits near the tail, each a real change. Hub and multi_support use the P1 choice. Chain disables tail rules downward and enables them upward, so each chain commit flips one node; hub and chain check exactly one L0 flip per commit. `why_depth3_p99_ns`: WHY at depth 3 on the same revision after each commit.
  - `cascade_eager_ms`: median of 3 retractions of revision 0's base. `cascade_lazy_ack_ms`, `cascade_lazy_drain_ms` and `cascade_lazy_total_ms`: the same toggle with the default `Budget`, drained with `Drain::Full`, median of 3. `lazy_deferred` counts the toggles that left a frontier.
  - Work: `units` of the eager cascade, its `status_diff` and `l0_flips`, the shape's `incidences`, and `commit_flips`. Each cascade's L0 flips must equal a count computed from the generated batches alone. The lazy `Settled.status_diff` must equal the eager one, and so must its `units`.
- **Interleaved runs:** `scripts/p11-ablation.sh run <results> <runs> base=<path/to/p11_ablation> cand=<path>…`. For each run and shape, the script runs the binaries in the order given (A, B, A, B, …). It appends each line prefixed by `<label> run=<k>`, with an `uptime` line before each process. It refuses to start while the 1-minute load average exceeds half the cores; `--force` overrides that. `scripts/p11-ablation.sh summary <results>` prints, per shape, metric and label, the median, min, max and max/min, and the error and failed-run totals. The noise band is the baseline's max/min.
- **G5**, one item per process: `p11_g5 <item> [n=1000000] [hub|chain]`. Stores use file projections and `StdDisk` with full sync, as ctxd opens them; the daemon items start ctxd in process with the default `[maintenance]`.
  - `cascade`: the store's ack of a base retraction (`ack_ms`, `ack_max_ms`), its op_log frame (`frame_max_bytes`), `settle_ms`, and `total_ms = ack + settle` against `eager_ms`, the same toggle on the store opened eager. Each of the 3 samples reopens the store lazy, then eager. The line prints the three budget verdicts. Work: the projection's diff rows for each settled epoch must equal the shape's flips.
  - `seeding`: the ack of `Db::commit_lazy` with `Budget { units: 1, .. }` for a base toggle on the shape (`base_seed_ms`) and for a SetHead on a record that 10⁵ rules cite through Sem (`sem_seed_ms`). Work: the drained diff equals the expected flips and an eager copy's diff, and the Sem SetHead changes at least 10⁵ statuses.
  - `next_ack`: `cascade_ack_ms`, then a small write's ack right after a cascade (`next_ack_ms`), and after the idle drain finished (`idle_drain_ms`, `next_ack_after_idle_ms`). It also reports `clone_fallbacks`, `requeued` and `replay_mismatches`; a mismatch is an error.
  - `structural_unknown`: 5 cascades. During each, up to 200 Certified reads of random `shape/k` records. `structural_unknown_fraction` is the share that answered PENDING(StructuralUnknown), next to `certified` and `not_certified_other`. Any other answer is an error.
  - `gc`: n revisions, 25 % of them superseded, then a 10,000-epoch keep window. `gc_plan_ms` is phase 1 and `gc_apply_ms` is phase 2. Work: the candidates and the collected payloads must equal the superseded count, with none rechecked out.
  - `reopen`: `reopen_ms`, the `Store::open` time after the load and 3 cascades. The reopened epoch must equal the epoch before.
- Both binaries take an optional `E` (and `p11_g5` a `read_settle_ms`) after the listed arguments, so a small smoke run can defer a cascade. Measurement runs leave them at their defaults.
